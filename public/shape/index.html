VSEPR Theory Foundations

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion Rules & Molecular Symmetry

1. Electron Repulsion Hierarchy

Lone pairs possess greater spatial volume and spread, exerting stronger repulsion: Lone Pair - Lone Pair (LP-LP) > Lone Pair - Bond Pair (LP-BP) > Bond Pair - Bond Pair (BP-BP). This compresses adjacent bond angles.

2. Steric Number (SN) & Hybridization

Steric number determines the electron geometry: SN=2 (sp, 180°), SN=3 (sp², 120°), SN=4 (sp³, 109.5°), SN=5 (sp³d, 90°/120°), SN=6 (sp³d², 90°).

3. Expanded Octet Rules (SN = 5 & 6)

For SN=5 (Trigonal Bipyramidal), lone pairs always occupy EQUATORIAL positions (120° apart) to minimize 90° repulsions. For SN=6 (Octahedral), two lone pairs occupy TRANS positions (180° apart) to yield square planar geometry.

4. Molecular Polarity & Dipole Moments

A molecule is polar only if polar bonds do NOT vectorially cancel out through molecular symmetry. Highly symmetric geometries (AX2, AX3, AX4, AX5, AX6, AX2E3, AX4E2) are non-polar when terminal atoms are identical.
